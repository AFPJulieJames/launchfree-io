The Best Launch Platforms for Side Projects and Small Launches

Big launch platforms can feel intimidating when it's just you and a side project. These are the friendlier places built for small, scrappy launches.

By Julie James, founder of LaunchFree.io· Last updated September 2, 2026· 3 min read

Side projects and small launches do best starting in community-first platforms like MicroLaunch, TinyLaunch, and Indie Hackers, then a fair, daily-capped launch day on sites like Uneed or Fazier, topped off with a permanent directory listing. None of that requires a big audience or a stadium-sized launch to work.

Where should a small launch actually start?

These are built around people who actually care about small projects: MicroLaunch, made for micro-SaaS with daily caps so a launch isn't buried; TinyLaunch, a lightweight, less-intimidating launch for early projects; Indie Hackers, where founders reply with real feedback; and Makerlog and WIP, build-in-public communities where shipping streaks and progress attract supporters before launch even happens.

A smaller, focused community often converts better than a giant feed. Twenty people who care about small tools beat a thousand who scroll past.

Frequently asked

Where should a side project or small launch actually launch?
Start with communities built for small projects: MicroLaunch, TinyLaunch, Indie Hackers, and build-in-public spaces like Makerlog and WIP. They're friendlier than the big feeds and give solo launches genuine attention. Add a permanent directory listing for lasting discovery.
Is Product Hunt too big for a small launch?
Not too big, but easy to get lost in without an audience already built. Many small launches get better results from smaller, focused platforms first, then use a bigger launch day once they have some momentum and supporters.
Do side projects benefit from directory listings?
Yes. A permanent listing gives even a tiny project a discoverable, indexed home and often a backlink, which keeps working long after attention has moved to whatever gets built next.
What's different about a fair-shot launch platform?
A fair-shot platform caps how many launches compete on a given day, so a small project isn't buried under a hundred VC-backed products. Uneed, Fazier, and MicroLaunch all use this model.
Should a small launch skip Product Hunt entirely?
Not necessarily. It's often better used as a second step once a project has some momentum from smaller communities, rather than the first and only launch attempt.
What happens to a side project's listing after it's abandoned?
A good permanent listing stays indexed and discoverable regardless, so the project can still find users, collaborators, or buyers later, with no maintenance required to keep working.
