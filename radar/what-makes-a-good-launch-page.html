What Makes a Good Product Launch Page (With a Simple Template)

A launch page has one job: turn a curious visitor into a user. Here's the simple structure that does it, and the mistakes that quietly kill conversions.

By Julie James, founder of LaunchFree.io· Last updated September 2, 2026· 3 min read

A good launch page has one job: turn a curious visitor into a user, through a clear headline, one primary above-the-fold CTA, real proof, and a benefit-led explanation, not a feature dump. Most pages fail because they try to do more than that one job at once.

What's the simple structure that works?

SectionWhat it does
HeadlineWhat it is and who it's for, in plain words
SubheadOne line on the benefit or how it works
Primary CTAOne action, above the fold, impossible to miss
ProofA short demo, screenshots, or early testimonials
The "why"Two or three short sections on the benefit, not features
Closing CTARepeats the ask for people who scrolled

What's a headline template you can steal?

[Product] helps [who] do [outcome] without [the annoying part].

Example: "LaunchFree.io helps founders launch their product for free without paywalls or approval queues."

What mistakes quietly kill conversions?

  • Clever over clear. If a visitor can't tell what you do in five seconds, they leave.
  • Too many CTAs. Five buttons is the same as no button. Pick the one action that matters.
  • Feature lists with no benefit. People buy outcomes, not specs. Say what it does for them.
  • No proof. A screenshot or a short clip does more than a paragraph of adjectives.
  • Slow or broken on mobile. Most launch traffic is on a phone.

Frequently asked

What should a launch page include?
A clear headline that states what it is and who it's for, a supporting subhead, a single primary call to action above the fold, proof (a demo, screenshots, or early testimonials), a short explanation of the benefit, and an easy next step. Everything else is optional.
What's the most common launch page mistake?
Being clever instead of clear. Vague, jargon-filled headlines that don't say what the product does lose visitors in seconds. Say plainly what it is and who it helps before you try to be memorable.
How long should a launch page be?
As long as it needs to be and no longer. Lead with the essentials above the fold so someone who reads nothing else still understands and can act. Add depth below for people who want it.
How many calls to action should a launch page have?
One primary action, repeated at most twice: once above the fold and once at the bottom for people who scrolled. Five different buttons competing for attention is the same as having none.
What kind of proof works best on a launch page?
A short demo, real screenshots, or an early testimonial. A screenshot or a short clip does more to establish that the product is real than a paragraph of adjectives ever will.
Is a good launch page enough on its own?
No. A launch page converts the traffic it gets, but it still needs somewhere to send that traffic from, like a permanent directory listing or community post. The page and the distribution channel do different jobs.
