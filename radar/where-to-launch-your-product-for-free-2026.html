Where to Launch Your Product for Free in 2026 (No Pay-to-Win)

An honest, curated guide to the best free places to launch your product this year, organized by what you actually need: reach, early adopters, or a permanent backlink.

By Julie James, founder of LaunchFree.io· Last updated September 1, 2026· 4 min read

The best free places to launch your product in 2026 are Hacker News, Reddit, fair daily-capped platforms like Uneed and MicroLaunch, and permanent directories like LaunchFree.io (The Runway). Each solves a different goal, reach, early feedback, or a lasting backlink, and stacking a few together costs nothing. No paywall, no approval queue you have to pay to skip, no "featured placement" upsell required.

This is an honest map of where to launch for free, organized by what you actually want out of it, not by who paid us to be listed (nobody did).

What should "free" actually get you?

"Free" is doing a lot of work in most people's search. Before you pick a platform, get clear on which of these three things you're actually after, because the best channel is different for each:

  • Reach: a spike of visitors and sign-ups in a short window.
  • Early adopters: a smaller number of the right people who'll try a rough product and give feedback.
  • A permanent backlink: a listing that keeps sending SEO value and referral traffic long after launch day ends.

A quick gut-check on any platform: does it use dofollow links, and does your listing stay up permanently, or vanish after 24 hours? Those two questions separate a lasting asset from a one-day blip.

What are the best free platforms for raw reach?

Hacker News (Show HN) is still a meritocracy with no paid placement. If your product resonates with a technical audience, a front-page "Show HN" can send thousands of visitors in an afternoon. Reddit communities like r/SaaS and r/startups run weekly "Share Your Startup" threads; you'll do far better sharing your story than pitching. Product Hunt remains the highest-reach launch day out there, and you can launch for free, just know the feed moves fast and the spotlight lasts a day.

What are the best fair-shot platforms with a daily cap?

A newer class of platforms fixes the "buried in the feed" problem by capping how many products launch each day, so every launch gets genuine attention:

  • Uneed: caps daily launches, free with an optional boost, suits polished tools.
  • MicroLaunch: built for indie makers and micro-SaaS, small but supportive community.
  • Fazier: daily and weekly leaderboards, free to launch.
  • TinyLaunch: a lightweight, less-intimidating alternative for early projects.

Where can you find free early adopters?

If your product is pre-launch or beta, you want feedback more than a traffic spike. BetaList has focused on early-stage startups since 2010 (free track with a wait, or pay to skip it). Indie Hackers lets you share with a community of founders who'll actually respond. Peerlist pairs a builder profile with a launch feature aimed at tech professionals.

Where can you get a free permanent backlink?

Launch-day platforms are a moment. Directories are an asset. A good directory listing keeps working for months: it's indexed, discoverable, and, if it's dofollow, it strengthens your domain authority. SaaSHub, AlternativeTo, and Launching Next all offer free listings.

How should you sequence a free launch?

You don't pick one; you stack them. A simple, zero-dollar sequence that works:

  1. Before launch day, get your site launch-ready and list on permanent directories so the backlinks start aging.
  2. Line up a fair-shot platform (Uneed, MicroLaunch, or Fazier) for your main launch day.
  3. Post a genuine "Show HN" or Reddit story the same week.
  4. Keep the momentum with the first-100-users playbook.

Should you ever pay to launch?

You never have to pay to be seen. Paid boosts can accelerate a good launch, but the foundation, a real product, an honest story, and a permanent home with a backlink, costs nothing. Start with the free channels, stack a few, and give your launch a runway instead of a cliff.

Frequently asked

Is it actually possible to launch a product for free?
Yes. Some of the highest-converting launch channels cost nothing, from Hacker News and Reddit communities to fair, daily-capped platforms and permanent free directories. A paid boost can help on some platforms, but it is never required to launch or to get real traffic and backlinks.
What is the single best free place to launch?
There is no single best; it depends on your goal. Hacker News and Reddit offer the most raw reach for the right product, fair platforms like Uneed and MicroLaunch give every launch a real shot, and permanent directories like The Runway give you a listing and dofollow backlink that never expires. Most founders use several in sequence.
What does 'pay-to-win' mean for launch platforms?
Pay-to-win describes platforms where paying money buys you higher placement, faster review, or visibility that free users cannot earn. Not every paid feature is bad, but on a fair platform your ranking comes from genuine interest, not your budget.
Do free launch listings give SEO value?
They can. A listing on a directory that uses dofollow links passes real SEO value and helps your domain authority. Nofollow listings still add discovery and referral traffic but pass no link equity, so it is worth knowing which kind you are getting.
Do I need a big budget to launch successfully?
No. A real product and an honest story matter more than ad spend. Free channels like Hacker News, Reddit, fair-shot platforms, and permanent directories such as LaunchFree.io can carry a launch entirely on their own, at zero cost.
Should I launch on multiple platforms at once?
Yes, but sequence them rather than dumping everything on one day. Get permanent listings live first so the backlinks start aging, then time your fair-shot platform and community posts around your main launch day.
